# An app account may sign in with the app's own Google client

Status: accepted, 2026-11-09. Amends [ADR-0011](0011-one-verified-account-core-for-creators-and-app-users.md). Besides a challenge the Kernel sends, a Google ID token for an address Google manages now proves an app account's email. Creator accounts do not change.

A small practice app moving onto Littlebox has staff who sign in with Google and do not want another password. ADR-0011 made a proven email the core of every app account, and password sign-in the only way in. The [plan](../plans/app-google-sign-in-and-s3-files.md) first proposed one Google client for the whole installation and accounts linked by Google's subject; building it showed both were wrong for Littlebox, and this record states what was built.

## Decision

- **Each app has its own Google client, set by its owner.** The owner makes a Web application OAuth client in their own Google Cloud console and sets it as two project secrets, `google_client_id` and `google_client_secret`, with `secret_set` and `use: "google"` or in Auth > Users. Those names take only that use, the values are never returned, and no route or call reads them. The installation has no Google client and no Google setting. `sign_in_methods_set` refuses `google` until both halves are set, and `secret_delete` refuses either half while Google is on, so turning Google off stays the owner's decision. Each refusal carries a `secret.*` or `signin.*` code with `fix: owner` ([ADR-0020](0020-a-refusal-carries-a-stable-code.md)); `kernel-rs/src/google-client-cases.json` holds them.
- **The whole flow stays on the app's host.** The owner registers `https://<app host>/_littlebox/auth/google/callback` in the Google client, and the same path on a verified custom domain ([ADR-0015](0015-a-deployment-answers-on-one-custom-domain.md)). `sign_in_methods_read` returns these as `redirectUris`, and `start` refuses a host whose callback is not one of them, so the URI Google sees is always one the owner was shown. A path address ([ADR-0024](0024-an-installation-names-its-hosts-from-one-domain-and-an-app-may-answer-on-a-path.md)) gets one more, `<domain>/p/<id>/_littlebox/auth/google/callback`, while the path is on. The name address starts its flow with that same id callback, so a rename does not break the Google client, and on the origin every path shares the flow cookie carries the Deployment's id in its name. The home address, `<domain>/`, has no callback of its own, so `start` refuses there with a sentence that says to open the app on its own address. The path host is never the editor's host, which the Kernel refuses at startup, so no other address needs refusing. `start` records the flow (its `state`, the ID token's `nonce`, a PKCE verifier, the Deployment and the app origin) for ten minutes behind a browser cookie. The callback takes the flow once, only on the same Deployment, origin and browser, exchanges the code with the app's client and opens the app's own session. No Kernel-host hop and no cross-host code exist.
- **Google's keys come from one fixed URL.** The Kernel fetches `https://www.googleapis.com/oauth2/v3/certs`, never a URL a token names, caches the keys by the answer's `Cache-Control`, and fetches again for an unknown `kid` at most once a minute. The token must name Google as issuer, the app's client as audience, an unexpired time, the flow's nonce and a verified email.
- **An account is matched by the email Google vouches for.** A Google sign-in reaches the app account that holds the same email, compared without case, whether it was made with a password or with Google. Google's word proves an address only where Google is its authority: an `@gmail.com` address, or one whose domain the token's `hd` claim names as a Google Workspace domain. Any other address is refused and signs in with email and password, because Google's own documentation says the `email` claim alone may not be unique or permanent. `google_vouches_for` in `kernel-rs/src/google.rs` is that one check. No identity table exists, so a package carries nothing new and the same person reaches the same account under another client.
- **A proven email takes an unproven account back, except the owner's.** Under local verification anyone can make an active account for an address they do not hold. A Google sign-in for that address replaces its password with an unusable one, ends its sessions and marks the email proven; recovery by email sets a new password. The owner's own app account is not taken back. While its email is unproven, a Google sign-in for it is refused with "This email belongs to the app owner's account. The owner opens it with Open as owner in the editor, not with Google.", and nothing changes. Once that email is proven, Google signs the owner in to it. Google never gives the role `owner` to another account.
- **Signup Policy still decides who may have an account.** For an email no account holds, the Kernel reads the Deployment's current policy in the transaction that would create the account, as the password path does, and refuses with the same words. An invitation accepted with Google needs the invited email and activates the account as the password path does. A suspended account is refused and a pending one keeps waiting for its email link.
- **Password sign-in stays on.** `signInMethods` is `["password"]` or `["password", "google"]`, so every account keeps a way back in. An account created through Google stores an unusable password hash, so an older Kernel that imports it treats it as a password account whose person recovers it.
- **The setting travels, the client does not.** A package carries `signInMethods` and the accounts. On another installation the Deployment keeps the setting and offers only email and password, and `sign_in_methods_read` returns a notice, until the owner sets the two secrets there and registers that host's redirect URI.

## Considered options

- **One Google client for the installation.** The first build used `LITTLEBOX_GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID` and `LITTLEBOX_GOOGLE_CLIENT_SECRET`. Rejected because the consent screen then names the operator, not the app, and because an app that moves keeps needing an operator who registers its hosts. The Kernel now ignores those variables and logs that it does.
- **A callback on the Kernel's own host and a finish step on the app host.** Needed only with one shared client. It was rejected with that client, because a single-use code crossing hosts added a store and a window that an app-host callback does not need.
- **Linked identities by Google subject, with no join by email.** Rejected because a package moved to another client would lose every link unless the subject matched, and because people who already had a password account had to sign in and connect Google first. Matching by an email Google is authoritative for keeps one account per person without a link table.
- **Accept any `email_verified` address.** Rejected because a Google account made with an outside address only proves that someone once read mail there.

## Consequences

- The editor preview has no Google sign-in, as it has no other Kernel route. It is a named divergence in AGENTS.md and `platform_guide`.
- An owner who wants Google on an app does a few minutes of work in the Google console per installation. Auth > Users shows each redirect URI with a copy button and says when either half is missing.
- An app account's `verified_at` can now come from Google as well as from a challenge. Under local verification, an unproven account whose address someone else proves with Google loses its password; the owner's account is the one exception.
